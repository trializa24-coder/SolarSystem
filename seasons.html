Science Grade VII · Chapter 7 · Earth and Solar System

The Seasons

Earth's revolution around the Sun, combined with a 23° axial tilt, causes the seasons to change. The season experienced by any region depends entirely on its position on Earth.

B · Earth's Revolution

Earth's Orbit & The Changing Seasons

"The change of seasons is greatly influenced by Earth's revolutionary movement around the Sun. When the Northern Hemisphere experiences winter, the Southern Hemisphere will experience summer." — Science Textbook Grade VII

C · Geography & Seasons

2 Seasons vs 4 Seasons

"Regions on the equator only experience two seasons: dry season and rainy season. Whereas regions in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres both experience four seasons: summer, autumn, winter, and spring." — Science Textbook Grade VII p. 215

🌏 Equatorial Region
INDONESIA · MALAYSIA · BRAZIL · KONGO
🌧 Rainy Season ☀️ Dry Season
Equatorial regions receive nearly vertical sunlight all year. The 23° axial tilt has minimal effect here, keeping temperatures stable — only 2 seasons exist, determined by rainfall and monsoon patterns.
DAYLIGHT ≈ 12 HOURS YEAR-ROUND · STABLE TEMP 25–35°C
🌍 Northern & Southern Hemispheres
EUROPE · EAST ASIA · AMERICAN · AUSTRALIA
🌸 Spring ☀️ Summer 🍂 Autumn ❄️ Winter
Regions outside the equator feel the full effect of Earth's axial tilt. When the Northern Hemisphere has winter, the Southern Hemisphere has summer — Australia celebrates Christmas at the beach!
DAYLIGHT VARIES 4–20 HOURS · TEMP RANGES FROM -30°C TO 40°C
D · The Main Cause

The 23° Axial Tilt — Figure 7.37

"Earth rotates with a 23° tilt relative to its rotational axis. This tilt is what causes the duration of day and night to vary across different regions." — Science Textbook Grade VII pp. 215–216

01
23° Tilt Stays Constant All Year
Earth's rotation axis is always tilted 23° relative to its orbital plane. The direction never changes — it always points near the North Star (Polaris).
02
June Solstice (±June 21)
N. Hemisphere tilts closest to the Sun → Summer in the North, Winter in the South. This is the longest day in the Northern Hemisphere.
03
December Solstice (±Dec 21)
N. Hemisphere tilts farthest from the Sun → Winter in the North, Summer in the South. Australia has summer on Christmas!
04
Equinox — Day ≈ Night
Occurs in March and September. Earth's axis is perpendicular to the Sun. Day and night are nearly equal (±12 hours) everywhere on Earth.
05
Only the Equator Stays Stable
"Only regions on the equator tend to have balanced day and night duration throughout the year." — Science Textbook Grade VII p. 215

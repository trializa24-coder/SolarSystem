SDGs & Our Solar System

The topics we study in this website — the Sun, the Moon, Earth, and the Seasons — are all connected to the United Nations' goals for a better planet. Here's how.

SDG 4 · Education SDG 13 · Climate SDG 14 · Oceans SDG 15 · Land Life
Earth is not just floating out there on its own. The climate, the oceans, the seasons, and the energy we use are all connected to the Sun, the Moon, and the way Earth moves through space. So when the United Nations talks about the future of our planet through the SDGs, solar system science is quietly behind many of those goals — even if it doesn't always get mentioned by name. On this page, you will see exactly which topics connect to which SDG.
Goal 4

Quality Education

Science Literacy STEM Access Digital Learning Curiosity

SDG 4 says that every person in the world deserves access to quality education. Science, especially space science can feel difficult or out of reach for many students. But when it is taught in a clear, visual, and interactive way, anyone can understand it and fall in love with it. This entire website is a response to SDG 4, making solar system science feel like it belongs to every student, no matter where they study.

🔗 Connected to these topics on this website:

🪐 Solar System
Interactive Planet Data
The Solar System page lets students click on each planet and explore real NASA data that makeing science feel hands-on and accessible, not just a page in a textbook.
☀️ The Sun
Simple Explanations of Complex Ideas
Concepts like nuclear fusion, the corona, and solar flares are explained in plain, simple English with vocabulary boxes — so students at any level can understand them.
🌍 Earth & Moon
Visual Learning Tools
Diagrams of Moon phases, eclipse types, and Earth's rotation help students who learn better through visuals rather than reading long paragraphs.
🌦️ Seasons
Local Context (Indonesia)
The Seasons page connects global science to Indonesian students' own experience — why Indonesia only has 2 seasons while Europe has 4, explained clearly.
📖 Curiosity about space is often what first pulls young people into science — and that curiosity can grow into a real career in research, engineering, medicine, or education. SDG 4 is about making sure every student gets that chance.
Goal 13

Climate Action

Climate Modeling Earth's Orbit Axial Tilt Solar Energy

SDG 13 calls for urgent action to fight climate change. To understand climate change, you first need to understand how Earth's climate works naturally — and that is 100% a solar system topic. The seasons, temperature zones, and weather patterns on Earth all come from how Earth moves around the Sun. On top of that, almost all of the data scientists use to track climate change comes from satellites orbiting Earth — technology that came directly from space exploration.

🔗 Connected to these topics on this website:

☀️ The Sun
The Sun Drives Earth's Climate
Everything we call "climate" — temperature, wind, rain, ocean currents — is powered by the Sun's energy. The Sun page explains how solar energy reaches Earth and drives the water cycle and weather systems.
🌦️ Seasons
Earth's Tilt Creates Climate Zones
Earth's 23.5° axial tilt is the reason different parts of the world have completely different climates. The Seasons page explains exactly how this works — and why it matters for understanding climate change.
🌍 Earth
Earth's Atmosphere & Greenhouse Effect
Earth's atmosphere traps heat from the Sun — this is the greenhouse effect. Without it, Earth would be frozen. But too much trapping causes global warming. Understanding Earth's atmosphere is the first step to understanding climate change.
🪐 Solar System
Venus as a Warning
Venus shows us what a runaway greenhouse effect looks like — 465°C on the surface. Comparing Earth and Venus teaches us exactly why controlling our own greenhouse gases is so important.
🌡️ Without weather satellites — born from space science — we would barely be able to predict storms, measure sea level rise, or track changes in global temperature. Climate action depends on space technology.
Goal 14

Life Below Water

Tidal Forces Ocean Cycles Marine Ecosystems Moon's Gravity

SDG 14 is about protecting the oceans and the life inside them. The Moon — which we study in detail on this website — has a direct physical effect on Earth's oceans every single day. The Moon's gravity pulls on Earth's oceans and creates tides. These tides are not just interesting to watch — they shape entire coastal ecosystems, control where fish swim, and support the lives of millions of people who fish and farm along coastlines.

🔗 Connected to these topics on this website:

🌕 Moon
The Moon Creates Ocean Tides
The Moon page explains how the Moon's gravitational pull creates high and low tides. These tidal rhythms oxygenate coastal waters, move nutrients around, and create the habitats that thousands of marine species depend on.
🌍 Earth
Earth's Water & Ocean Coverage
71% of Earth's surface is ocean — and the Earth page explains why liquid water exists here at all (the Goldilocks Zone). Understanding Earth's unique position in the Solar System is what makes ocean conservation meaningful.
☀️ The Sun
The Sun Powers the Water Cycle
The Sun page explains how solar energy evaporates ocean water, driving the water cycle that brings rain to land and returns nutrients to the sea. Without the Sun, the ocean would be a dead, motionless body of water.
🌦️ Seasons
Seasons Affect Ocean Temperature
The change of seasons — caused by Earth's axial tilt — affects ocean temperatures around the world. These temperature changes drive fish migration patterns and coral reef health throughout the year.
🌊 Moon-driven tides oxygenate coastal waters and create essential habitats for thousands of marine species that wouldn't survive without that constant daily rhythm. Fishermen in Indonesia time their work around the tides caused by the Moon.
Goal 15

Life on Land

Seasons & Biomes Magnetic Shield Solar Radiation Axial Tilt

SDG 15 is about protecting life on land — forests, grasslands, deserts, and all the animals and plants that live in them. But here's something many people don't realize: these ecosystems only exist because of how Earth is positioned and tilted in space. The diversity of life on Earth — from rainforests near the equator to frozen tundra near the poles — is a direct result of solar system science.

🔗 Connected to these topics on this website:

🌦️ Seasons
Axial Tilt Creates Different Ecosystems
Earth's 23.5° tilt creates completely different climate zones — tropical, temperate, and polar. The Seasons page shows how this works. Without this tilt, tropical rainforests, grasslands, and arctic tundra simply would not exist.
☀️ The Sun
Photosynthesis — The Basis of All Life
The Sun page explains how plants use sunlight to make food and oxygen through photosynthesis. Every food chain on land — from insects to elephants to humans — starts with a plant absorbing sunlight. No Sun = no land life.
🌍 Earth
Earth's Magnetic Field Protects All Life
The Earth page explains how Earth's magnetic field acts like a giant shield, blocking harmful radiation from the Sun. Without this shield, the solar wind would strip away our atmosphere and all life on land would be impossible.
🌕 Moon
The Moon Stabilizes Earth's Tilt
The Moon page explains that the Moon's gravity stabilizes Earth's axial tilt. Without the Moon, Earth's tilt could wobble wildly — causing extreme climate swings that would wipe out most land ecosystems as we know them.
🌿 Without Earth's 23.5° axial tilt, tropical rainforests, grasslands, and tundra simply wouldn't exist — every land ecosystem owes something to that lean. And without the Moon keeping that tilt stable, Earth's climate could swing wildly for millions of years.

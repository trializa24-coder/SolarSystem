Understanding the Concepts

What Are These Curricula?

Two curriculum innovations, one shared mission — to embed environmental responsibility into the heart of education.

🌊 Blue Curriculum

The Ocean in Every Lesson

Blue Curriculum is an educational approach that integrates ocean literacy and marine environmental awareness into formal and informal learning. It emerged from the growing recognition that the ocean — covering 71% of Earth's surface — is severely underrepresented in school curricula.


Developed through global initiatives such as the UN Ocean Decade and UNESCO's work on ocean literacy, it encourages students to understand how oceans regulate climate, sustain biodiversity, and connect communities. At its core, Blue Curriculum believes every learner should be ocean-literate — not just students living by the coast.

🌿 Green / Greening Curriculum

Sustainability Woven Into Learning

Green — or "Greening" — Curriculum is the process of embedding sustainability, ecological thinking, and environmental values into existing subjects and school systems. It's not a standalone subject; it's a lens through which all disciplines are reframed.


Grounded in Education for Sustainable Development (ESD), endorsed by UNESCO, this approach transforms how science, social studies, and even mathematics are taught — asking learners not just "what is it?" but "what does it mean for the future of our planet?" It's about turning classrooms into launchpads for environmental action.

Indonesian Curriculum Context

How Does It Connect to Us?

Indonesia's curriculum has been evolving — and the connections to Blue and Green ideas are already there, waiting to be deepened.

🏛️ Policy Level

Indonesia's Kurikulum Merdeka (2022) introduces project-based learning with one of its core themes explicitly focused on the environment — "Gaya Hidup Berkelanjutan" (Sustainable Lifestyle). This is a clear policy-level alignment with Green Curriculum principles, encouraging students to take real-world environmental action within their communities.

At the national level, Indonesia is a signatory to the UN's 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, and the Ministry of Education has incorporated ESD principles into national education frameworks. However, ocean literacy remains largely implicit rather than systematically embedded — a gap that Blue Curriculum directly addresses.

📚 Science Curriculum Connection

Science Content → Blue
Topics like ecosystems, water cycles, and biodiversity already appear in Indonesian science subjects — but rarely framed through an ocean-literacy lens. Blue Curriculum calls for this reframing.
Science Content → Green
Climate, energy, and environmental degradation topics in IPA (Natural Science) align directly with Green Curriculum's goal of sustainability-integrated learning.
The Gap
Much of this content is taught informationally rather than transformatively. The shift from "knowing about the environment" to "acting for the environment" is precisely what these curricula demand.
Opportunity
As an archipelago nation with the world's second-largest marine biodiversity, Indonesia has every reason — and responsibility — to lead the way in Blue and Green curriculum integration.
Implementation in Education

So, How Do We Start?

Change doesn't need a revolution — it starts in the classroom, the backyard, and the community. Here's how each of us can take part.

👩‍🏫 For Teachers

Reframe Your Lessons

  • Connect science topics to local environmental issues (e.g., coral bleaching, deforestation)
  • Design P5 projects around ocean or sustainability themes
  • Use inquiry-based learning: "Why is this disappearing?"
  • Bring in local environmental data — rivers, coasts, air quality
🧑‍🎓 For Students

Become the Change Agent

  • Start a campus eco-audit — what does your school waste?
  • Join or start a marine/nature awareness club
  • Document local environmental changes through photo journals
  • Turn science projects into real community proposals
🌏 For Citizens

Everyday Actions Count

  • Reduce single-use plastic — especially near waterways
  • Participate in coastal or river clean-up events
  • Support schools implementing Adiwiyata (Eco-School) programs
  • Stay informed — read about SDG 14 & 15 progress in Indonesia
🏫 For Schools

Build the Green & Blue Culture

  • Apply for Adiwiyata certification — make sustainability structural
  • Integrate ocean topics across subjects, not just science
  • Partner with local NGOs or research institutions
  • Create school gardens, composting systems, or rainwater harvesting

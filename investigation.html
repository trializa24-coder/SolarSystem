Step 1 · Real-Life Problem
Something You've Probably Noticed
🤔
Think about this...

Have you ever looked up at the sky on a hot summer afternoon and noticed the Sun feels almost directly above you? Then in winter, even when it's still daytime, the Sun seems much lower in the sky — like it's hiding near the horizon?

That's not just your imagination. The Sun really does appear in different positions depending on the time of year. And that difference actually controls whether you feel warm or cold outside!

💬 So here's the big question: Why does the Sun appear at different heights in the sky throughout the year?

Step 2 · Observe
Use Stellarium to Watch the Sky

Stellarium is a free app that lets you look at the sky on any date you choose — even dates in the past or future. We're going to use it to compare how the Sun looks on two very important days of the year.

🌐 Open Stellarium Web
🚀 Launch Stellarium →

Once it's open, follow these steps:
① Click Location and type your city
② Click Date & Time and set the date
③ Look at where the Sun is — is it high up or low near the edge?

☀️
June 21
The longest day of the year. Notice how high the Sun gets!
❄️
December 21
The shortest day. Watch where the Sun sits compared to June.

Step 3 · Question
What Did You Actually See?

Good scientists don't just look — they write down what they see. Now it's your turn!

Your Observation
What difference did you notice in the Sun's position between June 21 and December 21? Describe it in your own words — there's no wrong answer here!

Step 4 · Hypothesis
What's Your Best Guess?

A hypothesis is your educated guess — what you think is causing something before you fully investigate. Don't worry about being wrong. Scientists make guesses all the time, and that's how learning starts!

Your Hypothesis
What do you think causes the difference in the Sun's position during different seasons? Pick the one that makes the most sense to you!
🌍Earth is tilted on its axis as it goes around the Sun
📏Earth moves closer to or farther away from the Sun
🌕The Moon's movement affects how much sunlight Earth gets
